# Reciprocity

## What is it?

Reciprocity is the idea that people often feel an obligation to return a favor after receiving something from someone else. In persuasion, this principle can be used by providing something useful or valuable first, which may make a person more willing to respond positively to a later request.

## How do you recognize or use it?

Reciprocity can be recognized when a person or organization gives something before asking for something in return. This could include a free sample, useful information, a free trial, or a small gift. In design and marketing, reciprocity can be used by offering genuine value to the audience before presenting a call to action.

The principle works best when what is given is useful and does not feel like a forced exchange. The goal is to create a sense of goodwill that can encourage the audience to respond positively.

## Examples

### Free Sample

A food company could offer customers a free sample of a new product at a grocery store. After trying the sample, customers may be more willing to purchase the full product. The company provides something of value first, while the customer has the choice of whether to make a purchase.

### Website Application

A website could provide a useful free guide without requiring a purchase. After the visitor receives the guide, the website could present a call-to-action button such as **"Explore Our Products."** This applies reciprocity by giving the visitor something useful before asking them to take another action.
