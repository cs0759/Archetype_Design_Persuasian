## What Is Scarcity?

Scarcity is a principle of persuasion in which people tend to place greater value on opportunities, products, or experiences that are limited or difficult to obtain.

When something is available only for a short period or in a limited quantity, people may feel a stronger desire to act before the opportunity disappears.

This principle is often associated with the fear of missing out (FOMO), which can influence purchasing decisions and other behaviors.

## How Is Scarcity Used in Web Design?

Websites commonly use scarcity to encourage visitors to make decisions more quickly.

Examples include limited-stock notifications, countdown timers, exclusive offers, limited-edition products, and registration deadlines.

Scarcity can be particularly effective in e-commerce, event registration, and travel booking websites, where availability may genuinely be limited.

## Example 1: Online Shopping

An online clothing store could display a message such as "Only 3 left in stock" beside a popular jacket.

A customer who is interested in purchasing the jacket might decide to buy it sooner because they believe it may sell out.

The limited availability increases the perceived urgency of the purchase.

## Example 2: Event Registration

A website selling tickets to a conference could display the number of remaining seats or a registration deadline.

For example, a message stating "Registration closes Friday" could encourage interested visitors to sign up before the deadline.

This uses scarcity by emphasizing that the opportunity to attend is available only for a limited time.

## Ethical Considerations

Scarcity should reflect genuine limitations rather than artificial pressure.

Websites should not display fake stock counts, misleading countdown timers, or false deadlines to manipulate visitors.

Accurate availability information allows users to make informed decisions while still communicating legitimate urgency.
