## What Is Social Proof?

Social proof is a principle of persuasion in which people look to the actions and opinions of others to help determine how they should behave. When people are uncertain about a decision, they may be more likely to follow what others are doing, especially people they consider similar to themselves.

## How Is Social Proof Used in Web Design?

Social proof is commonly used in websites and digital applications to build trust and influence user decisions. Designers can show that other people have purchased a product, used a service, or had a positive experience.

Common examples include customer reviews, star ratings, testimonials, user counts, and popularity indicators.

Social proof can be especially influential when users are unfamiliar with a product or are comparing several options.

## Example 1: Online Shopping

An online clothing store could display customer ratings and reviews underneath each product. A shopper deciding between two jackets might feel more confident choosing the one with many positive reviews.

The reviews act as social proof because the shopper uses other customers' experiences to evaluate the product.

## Example 2: Educational Website

An online learning platform could display how many students have enrolled in a particular course, along with reviews from previous students.

For example, a course labeled "Over 10,000 students enrolled" might appear more trustworthy to a new visitor because many other people have already chosen it.

## Ethical Considerations

Social proof should be based on accurate information. Fake reviews, misleading testimonials, and exaggerated user counts can manipulate visitors and damage trust.

Designers should present genuine user feedback rather than create a false impression of popularity.
