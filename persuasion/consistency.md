# Commitment and Consistency

## What is it?

Commitment and consistency is the principle that people tend to act in ways that are consistent with decisions or commitments they have already made. Once someone makes a choice or takes a position, they may be more willing to continue behaving in a way that supports that earlier decision.

## How do you recognize or use it?

This principle can be recognized when someone is encouraged to make a small initial commitment before being asked to take a larger action. In design and marketing, this might involve asking a user to choose a preference, create an account, set a goal, or begin a free activity before presenting another related action.

Commitments are especially effective when people make them voluntarily. A good application should help users follow through on choices they genuinely want to make rather than pressure them into an unwanted decision.

## Examples

### Goal-Setting Application

A fitness application could first ask a new user to choose a personal goal, such as exercising three times per week. After the user selects the goal, the application could offer a button such as **"Create My Weekly Plan."** The second action is consistent with the commitment the user has already made.

### Online Learning Application

An educational website could ask a visitor to select a subject they want to learn and begin a short introductory lesson. At the end of the lesson, the website could invite the user to create an account to save their progress. Because the user has already chosen a subject and started learning, continuing with the course is consistent with their earlier actions.
